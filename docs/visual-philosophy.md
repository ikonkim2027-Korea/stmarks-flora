# Field Atlas

Field Atlas treats observation as a disciplined visual act. Space is organized like a survey grid, but never cold for its own sake: large image fields, precise measurement marks, quiet captions, and strong margins create the feeling of a modern scientific object that has been meticulously crafted over countless hours. The composition should feel edited by someone at the top of their field, where every crop, border, and line weight has been judged with painstaking attention.

The visual language is built from specimen photography, calibrated contrast, and restrained chromatic signals. Deep ink, mineral off-white, chlorophyll green, pollen yellow, and clear sky blue form a palette that feels contemporary without losing its botanical origin. Color is not decoration; it is a system for status, habitat, urgency, and attention. The work must look labored over with care, as if every tone has been tuned by deep expertise.

Scale and rhythm come from alternating field evidence with analytical structure. A hero image can dominate like a plate in a museum archive, while surrounding data sits in thin rails, compact counters, specimen codes, and repeatable modules. Text stays sparse and exact. Words behave as labels etched into the object, never as paragraphs trying to explain the object.

Balance comes from tension between living matter and rigid classification. Organic photography may fill the viewport, but the interface responds with sharp edges, numbered rows, tabular spacing, and quiet asymmetry. The final result should feel like an artifact from an imaginary botany lab: systematic, modern, and emotionally precise.

Craft is the non-negotiable signature. Every alignment should feel intentional, every repeated element consistent, every empty space earned. The final interface should carry the polish of master-level execution, not generic product styling: modern enough to feel current, careful enough to feel trusted, and visually distinctive enough that the guide becomes an object in its own right.
